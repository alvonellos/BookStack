====== Connect ======

Little by little, I started reclaiming pieces of my life. I went back to work in small doses, trying to engage my mind with problems that had nothing to do with pain or trauma. It wasn’t easy. My concentration would fracture sometimes, leaving me staring blankly at a computer screen, the echoes of old memories ringing in my ears. But it felt like progress just to try.
There were new challenges, too. Simple errands felt overwhelming. Grocery stores were loud, crowded, and full of sudden noises that made my heart pound. I learned to shop with headphones in, music drowning out the chaos. I planned trips for odd hours when the world was quieter. These were the new terms of my life.
My writing kept me grounded. Letters, journal entries, scraps of sentences typed into my phone when panic flared up. Words were my way of carving order out of chaos, proof that my mind still had something to say even when it felt broken.
I also started connecting with other survivors online. Reading their stories made me feel less alone. It reminded me that healing wasn’t a straight path—and that strength could look like simply making it through another day.
Slowly, the fear loosened its grip. It never disappeared entirely, but there were days I could breathe without the constant sense of threat. Days when I could smile and almost mean it.
And through it all, my mantra remained, steady and unshaken: Never give up. Better for being broken.
