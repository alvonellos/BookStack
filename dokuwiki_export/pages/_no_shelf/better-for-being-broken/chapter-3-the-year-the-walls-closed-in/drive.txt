====== Drive ======

Slowly, I began piecing together a new routine. I forced myself to go to therapy even when every fiber of my body screamed to stay home. I practiced grounding exercises to pull myself back when the world started spinning. I learned to count my breaths, name objects in the room, and remind myself that the danger was in my head, not outside it.
My husband became my anchor. There were nights he stayed up talking me through panic attacks, reminding me that I was safe, that the noises weren’t threats, that I was still here. His patience kept me tethered to the idea that maybe, just maybe, I could find my way out of this storm.
One day, on a whim, I decided to try driving a longer distance. I went for gold. I bought a new truck in Colorado, and decided I wasn't going to pay delivery, I was going to drive that motherfucker across the US. So, I did. It was so much fun, I did it again a few years later. 
  But before then, it was challenging. I had to learn to drive again. My PTSD made me panic in a vehicle, so it was hard for me to even go to therapy. 
Moments like that became my lifeline. Tiny glimmers that reminded me I wasn’t completely lost. That somewhere inside me, the person I’d been was still alive.
I wasn’t the same Alex I was before July 28, 2022. I never would be. But I was starting to believe that who I was becoming might be someone stronger. Someone who could look at all the scars, physical and invisible, and say: I’m better for being broken.
