====== Pray ======

One of the hardest parts was realizing how invisible my injuries had become to other people. Physically, I was up and walking, and on the surface, I looked “better.” But inside, I was still fighting for every inch of peace. People would say, “You look great!” and have no idea that I’d spent the morning talking myself out of a panic attack just to leave the house.
I began learning how to set boundaries. I stopped explaining my pain to people who didn’t want to understand. I learned to say, “I can’t handle that right now,” without apologizing for it. It felt awkward and selfish at first, but over time, it became a small act of self-preservation.
There were good days. Days when I went for short walks and felt the sun on my face. Days when I made it through a work meeting without zoning out. Days when I laughed with my husband and remembered what joy felt like.
There were bad days too. Nights when the nightmares returned, vivid and suffocating. Days when the buzzing of fluorescent lights in a store sent me spiraling. Times when I’d freeze in the middle of a task, unable to remember what I was doing or why.
Through all of it, I held on to the idea that healing isn’t about going back to who you were. It’s about becoming someone new—someone wiser, more compassionate, and infinitely stronger.
And every night, as I lay in bed staring at the ceiling, I repeated my mantra like a prayer: Never give up. Better for being broken.
