====== Survival ======

As the months crawled forward, I realized that surviving wasn’t enough. I didn’t want to just exist in the aftermath of trauma—I wanted to reclaim my life. But the path was anything but clear.
Some days, I’d manage to drive to a coffee shop and sit there, trying to feel normal among people chatting and laughing over lattes. Other days, I couldn’t even leave my bed, because my mind insisted there were threats waiting for me outside the door. It felt like living two lives: one where I was determined to heal, and one where I was drowning in fear and noise.
I started writing more, pouring my thoughts into journals and letters. It became my way of battling back against the chaos. On paper, I could organize the tangled threads of my fear, grief, and hope. I wrote letters I never sent—to my mother, to friends who disappeared, to the person I was before the crash. Sometimes I cried as I wrote. Sometimes the words were the only thing that kept me grounded.
My body kept reminding me that it wasn’t done healing either. New aches appeared where old wounds were still mending. Scar tissue tightened and pulled. My leg swelled unpredictably, making it hard to wear certain clothes or shoes. And every so often, I’d catch myself staring at my reflection, trying to reconcile the person I saw with the person I remembered being.
But through all of it, my mantra echoed like a heartbeat: Never give up. Better for being broken.
And though I couldn’t see the end of the tunnel, I started to believe there might still be light waiting for me on the other side.
