====== Description: Better for Being Broken ======

For much of his life, Alex Alvonellos believed resilience was just another buzzword—until the day he lay crushed beneath a motorcycle, staring at the sky, certain he would never walk again.
The doctors said the damage was severe. Walking would be a miracle. But that was only the beginning. One devastating accident left Alex crippled, launching him into surgeries, nerve damage, and the terrifying possibility that his legs would fail him forever. And though the accident happened once, the battle to reclaim his mobility came twice over—two grueling journeys to learn to walk again.
In Better for Being Broken, Alex pulls back the curtain on a journey equal parts harrowing and inspiring. He shares what it’s like to feel your body betray you, to sit in silent rooms grappling with pain and despair, and to wonder if life has anything left to offer. But he also shares the moments of fierce determination—the first trembling step after surgery, the endless hours of grueling physical therapy, the mental battles waged in the quiet darkness of sleepless nights.
Yet this book isn’t just about physical recovery. It’s the story of a man who rebuilt his life piece by piece, far beyond his body.


As an IT professional, Alex wrestled with complex systems, demanding projects, and high-stakes problem-solving while fighting private battles with his health and spirit. Even when his body felt like it was breaking, his mind remained driven, creative, and fiercely determined to keep learning and building.


As a student, he clawed his way through calculus, finished his degree against all odds, and proved that even when life knocks you down, you can get back up and claim your future.


As a man of faith, he found God not in the absence of suffering but in its crucible, discovering grace, purpose, and a peace that transcends understanding.


As a creator and dreamer, he channeled his pain into innovation, starting new projects and envisioning systems that blend technology with humanity, hoping to leave a mark bigger than himself.


Along the way, Alex reflects on his unique upbringing—born in South Dakota, raised all over the world, and shaped by a childhood marked by constant change. He opens up about the profound grief of losing his mother to the hidden battle of alcoholism and depression, a wound as deep and lingering as any physical scar.
With raw honesty, flashes of humor, and an unflinching eye for truth, Alex invites readers into his world, revealing not only the story of his accident but the quiet, everyday moments where true resilience is forged:


The courage to show up for work when pain makes every breath an effort.


The discipline to keep moving forward, one excruciating step at a time.


The faith to believe new dreams are possible when old ones lie shattered.


The stubborn hope that tomorrow might be better, even if today is agony.


Better for Being Broken is a memoir for anyone who has stared into the abyss and wondered if they’d ever find the light again. It’s a powerful reminder that sometimes the worst things that happen to us carve out space for the best parts of who we become. It’s proof that while life may break us, we can emerge not merely whole—but better than we were before.
This is not the story of a man who never fell. It’s the story of a man who rose, again and again—and discovered that resilience isn’t simply surviving the breaks, but choosing to become better for being broken.
